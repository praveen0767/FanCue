# Why Wickets Disrupt Batting Tempo

A dismissal fundamentally interrupts the scoring momentum of a batting side by removing a set batter and introducing an unsettled incoming batter to the crease.

When a wicket falls, play pauses momentarily. The incoming batter must take guard, observe pitch behavior, and face deliveries under heightened fielding pressure with close fielders. Historically, scoring rates drop in the over immediately following a dismissal as the new batter establishes their rhythm and assesses bowling lines.

Furthermore, losing successive wickets in quick succession ("a cluster of wickets") severely curtails batting aggression by exposing lower-order batters and risking an all-out conclusion before the 50-over quota is exhausted. Capturing wicket timing in an interval explains sudden pauses in scoring rates through observable match facts.
