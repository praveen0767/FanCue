# The Over in Cricket

An over consists of a sequence of six legal deliveries bowled from one end of the pitch by a single bowler. Once six legal balls have been completed, the umpire calls "Over", play ceases momentarily, and bowling switches to the opposite end with a different bowler.

A bowler cannot bowl two consecutive overs from the same end or opposite ends in a standard match. While an over strictly requires six legal deliveries, illegal deliveries such as wides or no-balls do not count toward the six-ball tally. Consequently, an over may physically comprise seven, eight, or more deliveries until the full quota of six fair balls is delivered.

In FanCue interval analysis, whole-over boundaries serve as deterministic checkpoint points because fielding positions, bowling assignments, and match conditions reset cleanly at the conclusion of each completed over.
