# Striker, Non-Striker, and Strike Rotation

During an innings, two batters are on the field simultaneously occupying opposite ends of the pitch: the striker (facing the bowler) and the non-striker (positioned at the bowler's end).

Strike rotation occurs when the batters exchange ends. Batters change ends whenever they complete an odd number of physical runs (1, 3, or 5 runs) during a delivery. Conversely, completing an even number of runs (2 or 4 runs) or hitting a boundary four or six leaves the striker facing the subsequent delivery. In addition, when an over concludes after six legal balls, the bowler changes ends, meaning the non-striker at the end of the previous over faces the first ball of the next over.

Understanding strike rotation allows fans to follow how batting momentum is sustained, how tactical matchups against specific bowlers are managed, and which batter faces the majority of deliveries in an interval.
