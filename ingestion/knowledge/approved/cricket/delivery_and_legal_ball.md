# Deliveries and Legal Balls

In cricket scoring and laws, a crucial distinction exists between a delivery (any ball bowled toward the batter) and a legal ball (a fair delivery that counts toward the six balls required to complete an over).

A delivery is deemed illegal if it breaches the laws governing delivery fairness. If the bowler bowls the ball too wide for the batter to make reasonable contact from a normal batting guard, the umpire signals a Wide ball. If the bowler oversteps the popping crease, bowls an unpermitted beamer above waist height, or breaches fielding restrictions during the delivery stride, the umpire signals a No-ball.

Neither a wide nor a no-ball increments the legal ball count for that over. When an illegal delivery is bowled, the bowling side incurs a penalty run added to the team total extras, and the bowler must re-bowl the delivery until six fair balls have been bowled.
