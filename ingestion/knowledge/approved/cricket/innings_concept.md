# Innings in Cricket

An innings is the fundamental structural division of a cricket match during which one team bats to score runs while the opposing team bowls and fields to dismiss batters and restrict scoring.

In standard limited-overs formats such as One Day Internationals (ODIs) and Twenty20s (T20s), each team is allocated exactly one innings. In traditional multi-day first-class cricket and Test matches, each team is allocated up to two innings. An innings terminates when ten wickets have fallen (all out), the maximum scheduled overs have been completed, or in multi-day cricket when the batting captain declares the innings closed.

When interpreting a match interval, the innings index indicates which phase of the match is under analysis. Events from separate innings represent distinct match contexts and must never be combined into a single continuous interval.
