# Legal Balls and Completed Overs Accounting

In limited-overs cricket, match resources are finite, bounded by the scheduled quota of overs (50 overs in ODIs, 20 overs in T20s) and a maximum of 10 available wickets.

Accounting for remaining resources requires precise tracking of completed whole overs and remaining legal deliveries. For example, after 30 completed overs in an ODI, exactly 20 overs (120 legal balls) remain available to the batting side. Any non-legal delivery (wide or no-ball) extends the number of physical events without decrementing the remaining legal balls.

When evaluating a team's progress, expressing resources in remaining legal balls clarifies the feasibility of targets: requiring 48 runs from 30 balls demands 9.6 runs per over (or 1.6 runs per legal delivery), anchoring the fan's understanding in concrete resource consumption.
