# MCC Laws of Cricket versus ICC Playing Conditions

In cricket governance, a strict constitutional distinction exists between the universal Laws of Cricket maintained by the Marylebone Cricket Club (MCC) and competition-specific Playing Conditions published by governing bodies such as the International Cricket Council (ICC).

The MCC Laws of Cricket form the universal bedrock applicable to all cricket played worldwide across all levels. The 2026 Edition of the Laws took effect on 1 October 2026, defining core principles such as pitch dimensions, fair delivery definitions, dismissals, and conduct.

Conversely, ICC Playing Conditions are regulatory overlays tailored for elite international competitions (Test Matches, Men's and Women's ODIs, T20Is). Playing Conditions deliberately modify or supplement specific MCC Laws—introducing innovations such as Powerplay fielding restrictions, per-bowler over limits, Free Hits on no-balls, and the Decision Review System (DRS). When explaining a format-specific rule, FanCue must cite the applicable ICC Playing Condition rather than conflating it with universal MCC law.
