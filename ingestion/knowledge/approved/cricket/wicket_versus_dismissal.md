# Wicket versus Dismissal Distinction

In cricket domain modeling, precision is required when distinguishing between a batter dismissal and a bowler-credited wicket.

A dismissal terminates a batter's individual innings. The Laws of Cricket specify multiple methods of dismissal: Bowled (Law 32), Caught (Law 33), Leg Before Wicket / LBW (Law 36), Stumped (Law 39), Run Out (Law 38), Hit Wicket (Law 35), Obstructing the Field (Law 37), and Timed Out (Law 40). 

A wicket is credited to the bowler's personal bowling statistics only when the dismissal directly results from the delivery: Bowled, Caught, LBW, Stumped, and Hit Wicket. In contrast, fielding-driven dismissals—chiefly Run Out, Obstructing the Field, and Timed Out—count against the batting team's total wickets fallen, but are never credited to the individual bowler. Both events increment the team's total wicket count by one.
